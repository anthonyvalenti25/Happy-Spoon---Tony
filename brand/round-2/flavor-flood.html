01 · Idea

Every flavor owns a color.

Happy Spoon doesn't have one brand color. It has five. Whatever flavor we're talking about takes over the whole surface: the page, the post, the ad, the email. The logo turns into that flavor's ink. Tap a color in the top bar and this whole guide changes with it.

One flavor per moment.

Every screen, post and ad features exactly one flavor and is colored by it, edge to edge.

The lineup is the only crowd.

All five colors appear together in one place only: the lineup strip (and the shelf of tubs).

Big flavor, bigger numbers.

Every flavor shows its protein and calories, set big. The color makes people look; the numbers make them pick us.

02 · Logo

One color, always.

On a flavor flood the logo is a single color, always white, on every flavor. The full-color logo (cocoa and coral) is only for packaging and plain white pages.

07 · Voice

Numbers, science and jokes

Numbers go big.

Protein and calories are the headline, not fine print. Always per serving, with the serving size nearby (a 32 oz tub holds several). Until the recipes are final we say "high protein" and never make up a figure. Words like "high protein" and "low calorie" have legal minimums, so check them against the final label.

ExampleSalted Caramel. XX g protein, XXX calories.

A little science is welcome.

What's in the tub, how it's made, why it's high in protein. Plain words, one idea at a time, and only facts we can point to.

ExampleA high-protein yogurt base with dessert flavors on top.

A little playful is fine.

Jokes are welcome when they're about the food or the craving. Never about anyone's diet or body.

ExampleDessert for breakfast? We won't tell.

09 · Rules

The short version.

    Always

  • One flavor per screen, post or ad, full bleed.
  • Logo and type in white on every flavor.
  • Flavor names wide and huge; headlines condensed.
  • All five together only in the lineup strip or on the shelf.
  • Colors change with a smooth flood, never a hard cut.
  • Show protein and calories, big, on every product surface (per serving).

    Never

  • Put flavor colors on cream or gray backgrounds.
  • Use the full-color logo on a flood.
  • Use a separate "brand color" that isn't a flavor.
  • Add stickers, doodles, outlines or patterns.
  • Print a nutrition number we haven't confirmed.
