### Product and formulation notes

- **Our ask:** high protein, lower calorie, dessert flavors, and a base that isn't tart. In our kitchen tests the Greek tartness came through the chocolate.
- **Target:** about 20 g protein and 180 calories per 3/4 cup (170 g / 6 oz) serving.
- **His Greek yogurt:** made from non-homogenized whole milk with the cream on top. It's strained in cheesecloth, with no centrifuge and no thickeners. It ferments at a lower temperature for longer, so it comes out mild and not very tart, almost like cream cheese.
- **Texture:** he can change how long it strains to make it thicker or looser, from "spoon stands up" to a softer pour.
- **Protein:** his base currently tests at **18 g protein per 6 oz**. Straining out more whey raises protein, but add-ins dilute it again. Adding protein powder can make the texture gummy or toothpaste-like ("there is such a thing as too much protein"). The simplest route is his mild, high-protein base plus flavorings.
- **Sweeteners and protein powders** go in before pasteurization, which is the kill step. Some sweetener is lost in the whey when it strains, but sweeteners are cheap. Non-nutritive sweeteners like monk fruit and stevia work.
- **Regulation:** Greek yogurt is a Grade A product. The state has to review what we add and at what step. Commercially prepared, aseptic purées and compotes can go in after pasteurization.
- **Flavor trick:** a frozen yogurt client used a little real fruit plus a fruit essential oil or concentrate. The smell really pops when you open the lid, without diluting the yogurt or adding the cost of lots of purée. It can be all natural.
- **Format:** fruit-on-the-bottom style or pre-mixed both work. It depends on whether the inclusion stays mixed or settles.

### Production logistics and pricing model

- **Early sampling:** he can ship us plain base to flavor ourselves.
- **Production:** everything (make, flavor, package, seal) has to happen in his facility.
- **Who supplies what:** BRVN supplies the milk and common ingredients. We consign our unique ingredients and packaging, and he stores them.
- **Pricing:** a tolling fee per finished pound, whatever the packaging.
- **Per-unit cost:** he won't quote until after trials, though he could give a range. He wants us to set a **target price point** so he can work backward from it. Keeping the recipe simple keeps costs down: one batch, no extra cleaning cycles.
- He liked the Happy Spoon name.
