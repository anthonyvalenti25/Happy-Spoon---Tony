# Happy Spoon Manufacturing & Product Development Guide

Last reviewed: October 1, 2026

## Recommended approach

Happy Spoon should develop the product and search for a manufacturing partner in parallel. The immediate goal is not a full national launch. It is to create one commercially repeatable yogurt base, launch two flavors, validate shelf life and economics, and complete a small pilot run.

Start with these five contacts:

1. Purdue FEMI for formulation, testing, regulatory and co-manufacturer readiness.
2. Reykjavik Creamery for high-protein yogurt pilot and manufacturing.
3. Ninth Avenue Foods for a local Indiana manufacturing conversation.
4. ESJA Foods for high-protein private-label yogurt capabilities.
5. Culture Fresh Foods for cultured dairy co-packing and cup filling.

## Packaging strategy

Do not purchase containers before choosing the co-manufacturer. The manufacturer's filling and sealing equipment determines the cup diameter, height, rim, material, fill weight and compatible lid structure.

For the first run, request:

- A stock 5.3- or 6-ounce cup already approved for the manufacturer's line.
- A heat-sealed foil or film lid.
- A pressure-sensitive label or shrink sleeve instead of a custom-molded cup.
- Manufacturer-applied date and lot coding.
- A standard corrugated master case.

Ask whether the manufacturer purchases cups, lids and labels for customers or requires Happy Spoon to order from an approved supplier. Custom-printed cups can be considered after sales justify their larger minimum orders.

Potential packaging suppliers:

- Amcor — yogurt cups and foil lidding: https://www.amcor.com/products/food/dairy/na/yogurt-pudding
- Huhtamaki — paperboard yogurt cups; packaging@us.huhtamaki.com: https://www.huhtamaki.com/en-us/north-america/highlights/recent/yogurt_cups/
- Lidding Films — heat-seal lidding films: https://lidding.com/

The co-manufacturer must approve every packaging component and conduct a filling/seal trial before Happy Spoon places a major order.

## Initial product brief

Use this information in every manufacturer inquiry so quotes can be compared consistently.

| Field | Proposed starting point |
| --- | --- |
| Product | Refrigerated, spoonable, high-protein Greek- or skyr-style yogurt |
| Consumer promise | Dessert satisfaction with strong protein and controlled sugar/calories |
| Launch format | 5.3- or 6-ounce single-serve cup |
| Initial flavors | Chocolate Fudge and Cookie Dough |
| Later flavors | Cookies & Cream, Salted Caramel and Mint Chip |
| Texture | Thick and creamy, without chalkiness or strong artificial aftertaste |
| Launch market | Indiana and the surrounding Midwest |
| Certifications | Decide whether kosher, gluten-free, non-GMO or organic are commercially necessary |
| Volumes | Manufacturer to quote pilot minimum, first commercial minimum and minimum per flavor |

Protein, calories, added sugar and fat should initially be stated as target ranges. Final claims must be based on a commercial formula and appropriate nutritional analysis.

## Questions for every manufacturer

1. Do you work with pre-revenue startups?
2. What is the minimum pilot run and minimum commercial run per flavor?
3. Can you formulate or refine high-protein Greek/skyr-style yogurt?
4. Can your line handle cookie pieces, chocolate chips or cookie-dough inclusions?
5. What is the maximum inclusion particle size?
6. Which single-serve cup sizes run on your equipment?
7. Do you procure cups, lids, labels, ingredients, cultures and flavors?
8. What pilot, setup, changeover, storage and production fees apply?
9. Who owns the final formula and process specifications?
10. Which shelf-life, microbiological and nutritional tests are included?
11. What food-safety and quality certifications does the facility maintain?
12. Can you support cold storage or refrigerated transportation?
13. What deposit, lead time and annual commitment are required?
14. How are overruns, ingredient waste and packaging waste charged?
15. Can the plant support a two-flavor launch without requiring five separate minimum runs?

## Regulatory and quality checklist

- Manufacture only in an approved, inspected dairy facility.
- Confirm Grade A status and current standing on the Interstate Milk Shippers list when applicable.
- Establish a written product specification and approved commercial formula.
- Complete shelf-life and microbiological validation on commercially produced samples.
- Obtain final Nutrition Facts calculations or laboratory analysis.
- Review ingredient statements and required major-allergen declarations.
- Substantiate every nutrient claim, including "high protein," before printing packaging.
- Establish lot coding, traceability, complaint and recall procedures.
- Carry appropriate product liability insurance before commercial sale.
- Confirm refrigerated storage and delivery temperatures through the full cold chain.

## Immediate team decisions

- Assign one founder as the manufacturing lead and single point of contact.
- Agree on the first two flavors.
- Set target nutrition ranges and acceptable ingredient constraints.
- Estimate a realistic first-order budget and twelve-month unit forecast.
- Schedule the Purdue consultation.
- Send a consistent brief to at least five manufacturers.
- Review responses together before signing an NDA, paying development fees or ordering packaging.

This guide is a working document, not legal, food-safety or regulatory advice. Confirm requirements with the manufacturer, a qualified food scientist and the appropriate regulatory authorities before production.
